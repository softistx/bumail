# @bumail/queue

## 0.3.0

### Minor Changes

- [#45](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/pull/45) [`cd9345e`](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/commit/cd9345e22722337f9e26e1464df6d43cd7b952a1) Thanks [@SteveGT96](https://github.com/SteveGT96)! - Add `@bumail/queue/redis`: `RedisQueueStore`, the `QueueStore` contract on Redis through Bun's own `Bun.redis`, so instances of a server on several machines share one queue with no driver to install. `RedisQueueStore.open({ client | url, keyPrefix? })` takes a `Bun.RedisClient` (typed by its shape, `RedisQueueClient`, and never closed by the store) or a `redis://` URL, for which it opens a client of its own and closes it in `close()`. Every operation that writes — add (with `maxItems`), claim, renew, complete, reschedule, cancel — is one Lua script, run by `EVALSHA` and by `EVAL` when Redis lost it, so two instances never take the same item and a crashed instance's items are claimed again once their leases expire. The due items are in sorted sets, each item a hash and its message a string written byte for byte, every key under `keyPrefix` (`bumail:queue:` by default; no `{`, no glob character). One Redis, or a primary with replicas, not Redis Cluster; the guide spells out what its durability means (`appendfsync`, a failover losing acknowledged writes, `maxmemory-policy noeviction`) and the ACL a worker needs.

### Patch Changes

- [#43](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/pull/43) [`7991b38`](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/commit/7991b3827ff67d6ad6f0f7fe3026e0a3c247db66) Thanks [@SteveGT96](https://github.com/SteveGT96)! - A worker that stalled past its lease, while another worker claimed the item, delivered it and dropped it, no longer reads the missing item as cancelled and emits `delivered` events for what it sent: it emits a `LEASE_LOST` error, no outcome event and no DSN, so the operator learns the message may have been sent twice. The store keeps no record of who dropped an item, so the error names both: lost to another worker that finished it, or cancelled — whether the lease's expiry passed, or a renewal had been refused while the item was still in the store, held by another worker or by none. When the instances' clocks are out of step, an item another worker takes and finishes between two renewals, before this worker's own clock passes the expiry, still reads as a cancel: keep the machines on NTP. An item cancelled under a lease that still held is told as before — its outcomes on the events, no error, no DSN — and a renewal that finds the item gone no longer reports `LEASE_LOST`.

## 0.2.0

### Minor Changes

- [#40](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/pull/40) [`0a219fd`](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/commit/0a219fd5ff651e4ad35cd87cc49a17f175034e08) Thanks [@SteveGT96](https://github.com/SteveGT96)! - Add `@bumail/queue/postgres`: `PostgresQueueStore`, the `QueueStore` contract on PostgreSQL through Bun's own `Bun.sql`, so instances of a server on several machines share one queue with no driver to install. `PostgresQueueStore.open({ sql, tablePrefix? })` takes a `Bun.SQL` client or a `postgres://` URL; a claim is one `UPDATE … RETURNING` whose item a `SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` picks, so two instances never take the same item, and a crashed instance's items are claimed again once their leases expire. The tables, the `bun:sqlite` store's with the message as `bytea`, are made by `migrate()` or on first use, once however many instances start together.
  
  A reply's text cut at `limits.maxReplyText` no longer ends inside a surrogate pair, and a lone surrogate in it becomes U+FFFD. The queue now also cleans every reply's `host` as it cleans the text: control characters replaced, a lone surrogate as U+FFFD, cut at 255 characters.
  
  **Breaking for code that calls a `QueueStore` directly** (a store of your own driven by hand, or `MemoryQueueStore` and `SqliteQueueStore` called outside `createQueue`): every store — memory and `bun:sqlite` included, not only PostgreSQL — now refuses, as `INVALID`:
  
  - a reply in `complete`'s recipients that is not an object with a string `text` (`A reply is an object with a text`), or whose `status` or `host` is not a string (`reply.status must be a string`, `reply.host must be a string`);
  - a NUL or a lone surrogate in `from`, in any address of `to`, in a recipient's address, in `owner`, or in a reply's `text`, `status` or `host` (`… holds a NUL or a lone surrogate, which a store cannot keep`);
  
  and treats an id holding a NUL or a lone surrogate as unknown. `createQueue` refuses such an `owner` too. What `createQueue` hands a store always passes these checks.

## 0.1.0

### Minor Changes

- [#35](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/pull/35) [`65d6aee`](https://github.com/softistx/bumail/commit/65d6aee351ff0476a09226cefe38e53b21ce2aee) Thanks [@SteveGT96](https://github.com/SteveGT96)! - The first release of `@bumail/queue`, the outbound queue. `createQueue({ store, hostname, resolver })` takes a message (bytes, a string or a stream, already signed) with its envelope `{ from, to }`, keeps each recipient's state (pending, delivered, deferred or failed, with the last reply), and delivers it one session per recipient domain through `@bumail/smtp/client`: by MX (the default), through a smarthost for everything (host, port, auth, TLS), or by a route per domain, within a limit of items at once (`concurrency`) and of sessions to one domain (`perDomain`). A 4xx, a connection error or a timeout is retried with exponential back-off plus jitter, configurable, by default RFC 5321 §4.5.4.1's: 30 minutes at first, doubling up to 4 hours, given up after 5 days as `4.4.7` with the last reply kept; a 5xx fails at once. Delivery status notifications (RFC 3464, a `multipart/report` built with `@bumail/mime`) go back from the null sender for a failure and, once, after a configurable delay (4 hours); never about a message from `<>`. The original returned is its headers, or the whole message when asked and small enough, bounded; nothing a remote server says reaches a header field with a CR, an LF or a control character. Limits on the message size, the recipients, the queue size, the reply text kept and the DSN. Events per recipient (`delivered`, `deferred`, `failed`) and for `dsn` and `error`; `list`, `get`, `retryNow` and `cancel`; `start()`, a graceful `stop()` and `deliverDue()` by hand, with an injectable clock and sender. The `QueueStore` contract is built for several workers: an atomic claim with a lease (owner and expiry), renewed while an item is delivered and claimed again once it expires, `complete` per recipient, `reschedule`, `list` and `cancel`. Two stores answer it, held to one spec suite (concurrency and lease expiry included): `MemoryQueueStore` from `@bumail/queue/memory`, and `SqliteQueueStore` from `@bumail/queue/sqlite`, on `bun:sqlite`, shared by the processes of one machine, with messages in a table of their own. `enqueue` checks every address as exactly one `sendMail` takes (`@bumail/smtp/client`'s `isMailbox`: an RFC 5321 Mailbox with no source route, no control character (C0, DEL or C1), no `>`, no U+2028 or U+2029, no Unicode format character (`\p{Cf}`), no lone surrogate and no IPv4 literal octet above 255), and refuses a bare CR or LF, so neither can fail a domain's recipients at delivery; an address a store holds that `sendMail` would refuse fails alone, as `5.1.3`, and a sender it would refuse fails every recipient at once, as `5.1.7`. `createQueue` checks each smarthost (port, TLS mode, `auth` only with `tls: 'required'`), `mxPort`, `mxTls`, the `timeouts` and `deadline`; a route `sendMail` still refuses defers its recipients as `4.3.5` with an `error` event, not a bounce, until `retry.giveUpAfter`, when they fail as `4.4.7` with a DSN like any deferred recipient. A lease renewal that fails is reported and tried again. `retryNow` is `false` for an item being delivered. A DSN cuts every returned line at 998 bytes. An `SmtpError` is recognised by its name and code, so a second copy of `@bumail/smtp` is classified the same. A directory the SQLite store makes is 0700; an existing one keeps its mode. The queue sends what the app enqueues: the app must have authenticated the submitter.
